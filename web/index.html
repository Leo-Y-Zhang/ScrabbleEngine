      How to play here

      • Place tiles: click a square and type (click it again to go down), drag tiles from the rack, or tap a tile and then a square. Typing a letter you do not hold uses your blank.
      • Enter plays, Esc brings this turn's tiles back, Backspace removes the last one, Space shuffles, arrow keys move the cursor.
      • Words are checked automatically as you place tiles: a word that is not in the list cannot be played, so there are no challenges here. That differs from tournament play, where an invalid word can be played and challenged off.
      • Clocks are total time per player. With Overtime penalty, as in the NASPA rules of 1 December 2016, each started minute past zero costs 10 points, and more than 10 minutes of overtime loses the game (100 points off; the opponent is raised to a one-point win if needed). With Lose on time, the game ends when a clock reaches zero. Tilefish's thinking runs on its own clock.
      • Practice allows hints, take-backs and pausing; the game is marked as assisted if you use them. Play has none, and its clock keeps running if you switch tabs.
      • Plan ahead: while Tilefish thinks you can set out tiles for your next move; they stay where they are if Tilefish's word leaves the squares free, and are checked as soon as it is your turn.
      • As you place tiles every word the move makes is listed under the board with its points and bonus squares; a word that is not in the list turns red, on the board too.
      • Rack: Sort puts it in order (A to Z, then vowels first); a quick sideways flick along the rack shuffles it. On a phone, the magnifier (or a double tap on the board) zooms in so tiles are easier to place.
      • Levels: Beginner and Easy choose weaker moves on purpose, for learning; Casual, Strong and Champion play their best.
      • Practice rates each of your moves once Tilefish has answered (switch it off under Advanced), and Hint helps in steps: a nudge first, then where the move goes, then the moves.
      • Puzzles: find the best move in one position; within 1% of Tilefish's choice solves it, with three tries. The daily puzzle is the same for everyone on the same word list. Your results and stats are under Games.
      • Unseen tiles counts vowels and consonants and warns about what matters (the Q without a U, the last S, the tiles in the endgame).
      • Hints and reviews come from Tilefish's search. Winning chances are estimates (simulation) unless marked solved; moves within about one percent are effectively equal. Reviews judge each move with only what its player could see.
      • Game review: each move gets a class by the winning chance it gave up against Tilefish's choice: !! Brilliant (the best move, giving up 12+ points of score for a better game) · ! Great (the only strong move) · ★ Best · Excellent (within 1%) · Good (under 3%) · ?! Inaccuracy (3–6%) · ? Mistake (6–12%) · ?? Blunder (12% or more). Where a game is settled or the endgame is solved, points given up count instead. The better move is drawn on the board, dashed, with its coordinate and an arrow for its direction; point at any move in the list to see it there, or click to keep it. Accuracy is the average over a player's moves (100 for Tilefish's choice). Try a move sets out your own move in any reviewed position and judges it. ← → step through the game, ↑ ↓ jump between key moves, B shows the best move, Home End go to the start and the end.
      • Notation: 8D WORD is across from row 8, column D; D8 WORD is down. Letters in brackets were already on the board; lower case is a blank.
